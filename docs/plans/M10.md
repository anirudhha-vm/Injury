# M10 plan — real public data (2026-10-21)

## Goal
Replace synthetic data with real, openly licensed data wherever a suitable public dataset
exists, retrain, and report results. No public dataset combines youth growth, video and
diagnosed growth-related injury (searched 2026-10-21), so each layer of the study is tested on
the best real dataset available for it, and the gaps are stated.

| Layer | Dataset | Licence / access | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Growth twin (H1, Paper B1) | Berkeley Child Guidance Study: 136 children (66 boys, 70 girls), height, sitting height (stem length) and weight every 6 months from 8 to 21 y; via the `sitar` R package on CRAN | GPL (>= 2) | Downloadable; used |
| Injury-risk pipeline (methods check) | Lövdal, den Hartigh & Azzopardi (2021) competitive runners: 74 Dutch runners, 7 years of training logs with injury flags, DataverseNL doi:10.34894/UWU9PV | CC0 | Downloadable; used |
| Smartphone vs motion capture (Paper A) | OpenCap LabValidation (SimTK, Apache 2.0) or BioCV (Bath, data-use agreement) | Login / signed agreement | **Needs the user**: create a SimTK account or accept the BioCV terms |
| Growth-related injury in youth | none public | — | Still needs the prospective cohort |

## Part 1 — growth twin on Berkeley
- Reference age at PHV per child from the *full* dense record (ages >= 4): Preece-Baines
  model 1 by non-linear least squares; cross-checked with a smoothing-spline velocity peak.
  Children whose record does not cover 2 y either side of the peak, or whose fit is poor, are
  excluded with a logged reason.
- Each child is then given only a one-season window (start age drawn at random among the
  measurement ages, so the cohort mimics baseline ages 8-17), as the twin would see in the
  study. The twin is fitted per sex to those windows; Moore 2015 (and Mirwald 2002 for boys)
  are computed from the same windows.
- Scores: MAE, bias, 90% interval coverage, by years from PHV; replicate window draws.
- Berkeley was not used to develop or calibrate Moore 2015 (PBMAS, HBS-III, Harpenden).

## Part 2 — injury-risk pipeline on the runners
- Week approach (three weeks of aggregated load predicting injury on the event day).
- Athlete-grouped 5-fold cross-validation (N3), athlete bootstrap intervals, calibration;
  plus a temporal hold-out (last 25% of each athlete's follow-up) and, labelled as a
  demonstration of leakage only, row-level random cross-validation.
- Models: intercept-only, penalised logistic, gradient boosting.
- These are adult runners and running injuries: a real-data check of the evaluation
  machinery, **not** evidence about growth-related injury.
