# M6 plan — maturity-conditioned movement encoder, neural hazard, uncertainty propagation

## Goal
The proposed model (C2 + C3): a small sequence encoder for screening trials whose features are
modulated by the athlete's growth state, a neural hazard head, and propagation of growth-state
uncertainty into the predicted risk — with the full ablation grid running end to end on
synthetic data.

## Files
- `src/youthtwin/sim/movement_sim.py` (extend) — per-session synthetic trial sequences whose
  shape depends on an athlete trait and on maturity, generated with `skeleton_sim`, so the
  encoder has something real to learn. The injury simulation's load proxy is tied to it.
- `src/youthtwin/models/encoder/sequences.py` — trial → fixed-length multi-channel array
  (time-normalised joint angles and angular velocities); per-channel normalisation statistics
  fitted on training athletes only.
- `src/youthtwin/models/encoder/film.py` — FiLM layer: `gamma(g) * h + beta(g)`.
- `src/youthtwin/models/encoder/tcn.py` — dilated residual 1D CNN (≤ 200k parameters).
- `src/youthtwin/models/encoder/stgcn.py` — spatio-temporal graph convolution on the joint graph.
- `src/youthtwin/models/encoder/conditioning.py` — `none | concat_input | late_fusion | film`
  as one switch around either backbone; attention pooling of trials into a session embedding.
- `src/youthtwin/models/pretrain/ssl.py` — masked-segment reconstruction on unlabelled trials;
  augmentations (time-warp, noise, small rotation; never left/right mirroring).
- `src/youthtwin/models/risk/hazard_neural.py` — MLP head on growth state + session embedding +
  tabular inputs; trained with athlete-grouped early stopping; deep ensemble over seeds.
- `src/youthtwin/models/risk/uncertainty.py` — risk distribution over growth-posterior draws;
  ensemble spread; optional split-conformal interval.
- `scripts/ablation.py` — runs `configs/eval/ablations.yaml` (nine configurations) and writes
  one tidy results table with run IDs. Results come only from runs.
- `configs/models/*.yaml` — architecture and training settings.

## Tests
- shapes and parameter budget; each conditioning mode trains for a few steps and its loss falls;
- FiLM with identity parameters equals the unconditioned network; conditioning actually changes
  the output when the growth state changes, and `none` does not;
- normalisation statistics come from training athletes only;
- augmentations preserve limb identity (no mirroring) and stay within configured ranges;
- seeds reproduce training exactly;
- posterior-draw propagation: zero growth uncertainty gives zero risk spread; wider posterior
  gives wider risk interval; interval coverage on a constructed case;
- ablation grid runs end to end on a small synthetic cohort and yields one row per configuration
  with a run ID and no hard-coded values.

## Acceptance criteria (brief §7)
- Ablation grid runs end to end on synthetic data.
- Each conditioning mode trains.
- Ensemble + posterior-draw uncertainty produces calibrated intervals on synthetic data.

## Risks stated up front
- With about 55 growth-related injuries in the default synthetic cohort, differences between
  conditioning modes will be within noise. M6 shows the machinery runs; it cannot show that
  FiLM is better (hypothesis H3 needs real data or a much larger simulation).
- The simulation must contain a maturity-dependent movement effect for conditioning to matter at
  all; that effect is something I choose, so any "win" for FiLM on synthetic data would partly
  reflect how I built the simulation. This will be said plainly wherever results appear.
